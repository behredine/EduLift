Student Guide — How the Circulatory System Works

Use the simulator above as you read. Watch the red (oxygenated) and blue (deoxygenated) cells flow around the loop, and use the controls to slow the heart down, see inside it, or pull it apart.

1 · Two circuits, one pump

The heart is really two pumps in one. The right side pumps blood to the lungs (the pulmonary circuit) to pick up oxygen. The left side pumps the freshly oxygenated blood to the rest of the body (the systemic circuit). Every heartbeat pumps through both circuits at the same time.

2 · Follow one red blood cell

Start at the superior and inferior vena cava — the big veins that bring deoxygenated (used-up) blood back from the body. The route is:

SVC / IVC → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary valve → pulmonary arteries → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → mitral valve → left ventricle → aortic valve → aorta → the whole body

Watch a cell change colour as it passes through the lungs — that is where it drops off CO2 and picks up O2.

3 · Oxygenated vs deoxygenated blood

In the simulator, red = oxygenated (loaded with oxygen, leaving the heart for the body) and blue = deoxygenated (returning to the heart after the body has used the oxygen). In reality blood is always red — the colours just show how much oxygen it is carrying.

4 · Valves are one-way doors

Blood must flow in one direction only. The four valves open and close in rhythm with the beat: when the ventricles squeeze (systole), the aortic and pulmonary valves open to let blood out while the mitral and tricuspid close to stop it flowing backwards. During relaxation (diastole) the opposite happens. Press Pulse to freeze the beat and inspect a valve closely.

5 · The four chambers

Click Chambers to see them inside the muscle. The atria (top) are thin-walled — they just receive blood and nudge it into the ventricles. The ventricles (bottom) have thick muscular walls because they do the hard pumping. The left ventricle is the strongest of all: it must push blood all the way around the whole body.

6 · The lungs and the coronary arteries

The pulmonary arteries are the only arteries that carry deoxygenated blood, and the pulmonary veins are the only veins carrying oxygenated blood. The coronary arteries are the heart's own blood supply — the heart muscle needs oxygen too, and if a coronary artery becomes blocked, part of the heart is starved of oxygen (a heart attack).

Key terms to remember: pulmonary and systemic circuits, atria and ventricles, systole and diastole, aortic / pulmonary / mitral / tricuspid valves, oxygenated vs deoxygenated blood, coronary arteries, capillaries.
